Mafia Island Marine Park

A world-class diving destination with some of the richest reefs in the world.

World-class diving

Mafia is renowned as an excellent world-class diving destination, with some of the richest reefs in the world. The park covers the southern part of Mafia Island and includes the inhabited islands of Chole, Juani, Jibondo and Bwejuu, and several uninhabited islets and the associated waters.

Where it is

Mafia Island and its chain of small islets lie about 120 km south of Dar es Salaam and 20 km offshore from the eastern edge of the Rufiji, one of the largest delta systems in Africa. To the east of the island is the Indian Ocean. The main island is about 48 km long and 17 km wide at its widest point, with several smaller islands and islets scattered to the west and south.

The marine park

Mafia Island Marine Park (MIMP) is one of eight small marine reserves along the Tanzanian coast established under the Fisheries (Marine Reserves) Regulations of 1975. Two of them, Chole Bay and Kitutia Reef, are now within the park.

The park covers some 822 sq km, more than 75% of it below the high water mark. It hosts an outstanding mosaic of tropical marine habitats, including coral reefs, sea grass beds, mangroves and inter-tidal flats. A remnant block of threatened lowland coastal forest survives along the eastern seaboard of the island, roughly half of it within the park boundary.

Two species of sea turtle use Mafia’s beaches as nesting grounds, and the area is internationally recognised as a critical site for biodiversity. Several sites of historic ruins lie within the park, some dating back to the 13th century.

Mafia’s separation from the mainland and its freedom from industrial development have kept its waters among the least contaminated in Tanzania. The park is nationally important as one of the few remaining reef complexes in Tanzania’s coastal waters in relatively intact condition.
