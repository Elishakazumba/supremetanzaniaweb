9 Days Lemosho Route

TrekKilimanjaro

About this route

The Lemosho Glades route is one of the lesser-used routes for the initial ascent of Kilimanjaro, partly because of its remote location and the difficult roads to the trailhead. This nine-day itinerary gives you plenty of time to acclimatise, with a full day on the Shira plateau and a short, important acclimatisation day at Karanga Valley.

Itinerary

  1. Day 1: Lemosho Trailhead (2,375 m) to Forest Camp (2,895 m)

    You leave Arusha at around 7:30 am for Londorossi Gate, about 4 hours away, where you complete the entry formalities. Another hour’s drive takes you to the Lemosho trailhead. On arrival you eat lunch, then start through undisturbed forest that winds to the first camp, only about 3 to 4 hours of trekking. You spend the night at Forest Camp (Mti Mkubwa Camp).

    Trekking time: 3 to 4 hours

  2. Day 2: Forest Camp to Shira 1 Camp (3,720 m)

    Once out of the forest, you take a steep track into a savannah of tall grasses, heather and volcanic rock draped with lichen beards. You climb through the lush rolling hills below the Shira plateau and reach camp at the edge of the plateau. The view of Kibo across the plateau is amazing.

    Trekking time: 5 to 6 hours

  3. Day 3: Shira 1 Camp to Shira 2 Camp (3,950 m)

    A full day exploring the Shira plateau. You trek east towards Kibo’s glaciated peak, with the option to visit the ancient collapsed Shira cone, the oldest of Kilimanjaro’s three volcanoes. You arrive at Shira 2 Camp (Fischer’s Camp). Shira is one of the highest plateaus on earth, averaging about 3,800 m.

    Trekking time: About 3 hours without the optional excursions

  4. Day 4: Shira 2 Camp to Moir Camp (4,205 m)

    You climb through the heather and into the barren high-altitude desert, towards the Shark’s Tooth, and on to Moir Camp. Moir is an isolated, seldom-used camp with views over the Shira plateau. A resupply of fresh food and supplies reaches camp in the afternoon.

    Trekking time: 2 to 3 hours

  5. Day 5: Moir Camp to Barranco Camp (3,950 m)

    You continue steadily uphill over expansive ridgelines of high desert to Lava Tower for lunch. Lava Tower, a 90-metre volcanic plug, marks an exposed pass at about 4,600 m. In the afternoon you descend the steep track into the Great Barranco Valley. Barranco Camp sits in a valley enclosed on three sides by massive walls and the Kibo massif itself. Hanging glaciers glint above amid eerie plants such as giant groundsels (Senecio kilimanjari) and the endemic giant lobelia.

    Trekking time: 6 to 7 hours (about 4 hours to Lava Tower)

  6. Day 6: Barranco Camp to Karanga Camp (3,995 m)

    A short trekking day to Karanga Valley Camp, between Barranco and Barafu camps, which has proven very important for acclimatisation. The day begins with a scramble up the steep Barranco Wall, then you traverse up and down the slopes of valleys carved into the mountain’s southern face. Kibo’s glaciers loom to your left and the jagged peaks of Mawenzi jut into the African sky straight ahead. After 3 to 4 hours you reach Karanga Valley and a hot lunch at camp. The afternoon can be spent relaxing or on excursions from camp.

    Trekking time: 3 to 4 hours

  7. Day 7: Karanga Camp to Barafu Camp (4,630 m)

    From here the trail turns steadily uphill. It grows colder and the landscape more sparse as you near Barafu Camp, set on a small, exposed flat area on a ridge. It is the base camp for your summit attempt at midnight tonight. Eat and drink as much as possible before retiring early to your tent to rest and sleep.

    Trekking time: 4 to 5 hours

  8. Day 8: Barafu Camp to Uhuru Peak (5,895 m), then Mweka Camp (3,110 m)

    A steaming hot drink arrives very early on this momentous day. A midnight wake-up call starts a long day of 12 to 17 hours of trekking, and you set off at 1 am. Temperatures range from just below freezing at midnight to between -12°C and -23°C just before dawn at the summit. In the early dawn light the winding path of endless switchbacks takes shape in the shadow of the Ratzel and Rebmann glaciers, and behind you the spires of Mawenzi are silhouetted against the red glow of the sun beneath the clouds. After about 6 hours you reach the crater rim at Stella Point (about 5,790 m), the end of the steep track. From there a gentle slope takes about an hour to Uhuru Peak. The strength it takes to climb this final stretch is, for many, monumental. After a short time at the summit you descend the steep scree trail to Barafu Camp for some rest, then summon your strength again for the further descent to Mweka Camp for the night.

    Total trekking: 12 to 17 hours

  9. Day 9: Mweka Camp to Mweka Gate (1,630 m)

    You descend straight to the gate, where you have lunch and are awarded your climbing certificates.

    Trekking time: 2 to 3 hours
