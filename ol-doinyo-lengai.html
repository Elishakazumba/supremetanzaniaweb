Mount Ol Doinyo Lengai

The Mountain of God

“Ol Doinyo Lengai” means “The Mountain of God” in the Maasai language. The summit of this strato-volcano is 2,962 metres above sea level and looks straight into the caldera of Tanzania’s only officially certified active volcano, and the world’s only carbonatite volcano. Eruptions have been recorded since 1883, and the largest deposited ash 100 kilometres away in Loliondo, near the Kenyan border to the north-west.

Lengai lies just south of Lake Natron in the Rift Valley, in the heart of Maasai country, and is regarded locally as a sacred mountain. The Maasai have long used it to pray to their god, Ngai. It rises about 2,878 metres above the soda ash flats of Lake Natron, about 120 kilometres north-west of Arusha. It is the only active volcano in the world that erupts natrocarbonatite lava, which is cooler (about 510°C) than basaltic lava (about 1,100°C) and contains less silica.

The view from the top

From the summit crater, the hot, barren salt flats of Lake Natron stretch away to the north. To the south lie the Crater Highlands and the Ngorongoro Game Reserve, with Kilimanjaro dominating the eastern horizon and the forested escarpments of the Rift Valley to the west. Lake Natron offers good nesting sites for flamingos, pelicans and geese, and more than 350 bird species have been recorded. The mountain often has minor eruptions that build cone-like structures on the crater floor, and it is possible to walk across the crater floor.

The climb

The ascent is demanding because of daytime heat, lack of water, steep slopes of ash and crumbly rock, and the large height gain. You normally start early and reach the summit at sunrise. Wear a warm jacket and long trousers, because the summit can be cold before dawn. The route from the north-west lets you descend early in the morning shadow. Unlike Africa’s two highest mountains, Lengai takes about six to seven hours to reach the summit crater.

Wildlife and culture

Walking with armed Maasai guides, you may see olive baboon, vervet monkey, monitor lizard, hyena, lion, leopard, jackal, Grant’s gazelle, impala and zebra. Nearby Maasai bomas give you the chance to meet the community and learn about their culture and traditions.
